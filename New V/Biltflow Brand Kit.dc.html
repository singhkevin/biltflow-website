01 — At a glance

The brand in one screen

Biltflow is not another project-management app. It is the connective operating layer for the whole development and construction lifecycle — and the brand has to feel like moving from chaos into control.

We are
  • An operating system, not a tool
  • AI-native from the ground up
  • Trained on real project reality
  • Integration-first, not rip-and-replace
  • Portfolio-level, not project-level
We are not
  • A dashboard you go and look at
  • A generic AI bolted onto old software
  • A humanoid chatbot mascot
  • Futuristic for its own sake
  • Loud, playful or exclamation-marked

02 — Positioning

What the brand has to argue

Three structural facts about the market drive every design decision. Keep them on the wall while concepting.

Fact 01
The market is wider than “Class 2”

High-density residential, mixed-use, hotels, serviced apartments and build-to-rent share one complex, software-intensive delivery workflow. Speak to all of them.

Fact 02
Nobody buys this alone

Around 40 roles evaluate a platform like Biltflow through different lenses. Executives want margin and control; site teams want speed. Every page needs a persona door.

Fact 03
Fragmentation is the opening

90+ products across 13 categories, split between suites, ERPs, point solutions and owner platforms. Show the fragmentation, then show the connective layer.

Positioning statement — use verbatim in briefs

Biltflow is the construction and development operating system that connects the tools you already run, standardises the way work happens, and puts a construction-trained AI workforce across the whole lifecycle — so every project makes the next one smarter.

03 — Voice & messaging

Short sentences. Full stops. No hype.

The voice is a construction director briefing a room, not a SaaS landing page. Declarative, specific, unhurried. Rhythm comes from clipped sentences and deliberate repetition.

Hero idea 01 — the founding line
The tools changed.
The way construction operates didn't.
Until now.
Hero idea 02
The next project already knows.

Give this line air. Never stack copy on top of it, never speak over it, never put a button next to it.

Signature line library
  • One system. All projects. All data. All connected.
  • Start anywhere. Connect everything.
  • Not disconnected AI tools. One coordinated digital workforce.
  • Connecting what happened to why it happened.
  • Build. Learn. Improve. Repeat.
Words we use
operating system lifecycle intelligence layer digital workforce orchestration AI-native module connector programme portfolio standardise in control
Words we avoid
revolutionary game-changing seamless solution empower cutting-edge next-generation AI-powered rip and replace unlock your potential

No exclamation marks. No emoji. No em-dash pileups. Say AI-native, never “AI-powered”.

Naming & spelling
  • Biltflow in running copy — never BiltFlow, BILTFLOW or Bilt Flow. All-caps only as artwork.
  • EVE always all caps. Agent names in Title Case: Tango, Cash, Sophia.
  • Australian English: organise, standardise, programme.
  • Programme = construction schedule. Never “program” in delivery copy.
  • Sentence case for UI labels and headings; Title Case only for named products and pathways.

05b — Light mode

Chalk — the same system, inverted

Dark is the brand's public face; Chalk is its working face. Everything that gets read for a long time or printed — documents, contracts, board packs, dense tables, investor decks — belongs here. The rules do not change: hairlines not shadows, one accent, tight radii. Only the ladder flips.

Use Chalk for
  • Printed and PDF documents of any kind
  • Contracts, reports, cost plans, registers
  • Investor and board decks
  • Long-form reading: guides, help, legal
  • Dense tables and exported data
  • Email — dark mode renders unreliably
Stay dark for
  • The homepage hero and the film
  • Any title card or brand reveal
  • The $5B+ credibility band
  • Product screenshots used in marketing
  • Anything where the blue has to be the only light in the room
The one real change

Ultramarine 400 fails on light. #6B72FF hits only 3.5:1 on Chalk 050 — fine for a chart fill, illegal for text or a link.

On light, the accent steps down: #1F27D6 for text and links (8.3:1), #2E38FF for button fills and larger accent type (white on it 6.7:1).

10 — Motion

Controlled, purposeful, never decorative

Before Biltflow, motion is chaotic and overlapping. After Biltflow, every movement means something happened: one click, one confirmation, one workflow completing. The transition between those two states is the brand's central gesture.

01 · Noise

Overlapping, accelerating, uncontained. Windows multiply, notifications stack. Deliberately uncomfortable.

02 · Silence

Everything stops at once. No fade, no easing out — a hard cut to black, held longer than feels comfortable.

03 · Signal

One element moves at a time, along a single path, and resolves. Movement equals meaning.

Duration & easing
120msHover, focus, chip toggle
200msPanel expand, tab change, row select
320msRoute change, modal, drawer
600msData flowing along the lifecycle line
1200msHero reveal only — once per page
ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)
ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)
no spring · no bounce · no overshoot
Permitted moves — hover to test

On the website, three more are allowed: scroll-reveal (fade + ≤28px rise), the lifecycle line drawing itself into view, and gentle parallax on full-bleed photographs.

Border and tint shift — the default hover
Opacity fade — de-emphasis only
Short directional slide — max 8px

Honour prefers-reduced-motion: drop to opacity only.

Never animate
  • The logo — it appears, it does not assemble, explode or particle-in
  • Parallax on type or layered UI, and any scroll-jacking (gentle parallax on large photographs, ≤5% travel, is fine)
  • Counters ticking up on every stat
  • Looping background gradients, meshes or “AI” shimmer
  • Card lift-and-shadow on hover
  • More than one element at a time in a single frame — scroll-reveals may stagger by ≤90ms, nothing else overlaps
11 — Imagery & art direction

Real construction. Real UI. Nothing synthetic.

Credibility is the entire visual strategy. Every image is either a genuine construction environment or a genuine Biltflow screen — the audience has to believe the product exists before they understand what it does.

Canonical art direction — hero film storyboard
The tonal arc every asset inherits: archival grain and warmth → fragmentation → black → cool, spacious, precise product. Match this grade in photography, decks and web imagery.
Before Biltflow — the problem
  • Warmer, grainier, slightly desaturated
  • Handheld, crowded frames; people mid-task
  • Paper, phones, screens overlapping
  • Never beautifully composed — fragmentation is the point
After Biltflow — the product
  • Cool, clean, high-contrast, generous negative space
  • Locked-off framing; one subject per frame
  • Real screens at real resolution — no faked UI
  • Blue is the only colour that moves
Photography rules
  • Australian high-density sites: towers, façades, cores, formwork, fit-out
  • Real practitioners in real environments — no staged smiles at camera
  • Hard hats and hi-vis appear because the site requires them, not as props
  • Finished-building imagery is warmer: lobbies, apartments, residents
  • Scrim any photo carrying type: rgba(0,0,4,.7)
Banned imagery
Glowing brains, neural networks, circuit boards, humanoid robots

13 — Quick reference

The eight decisions that keep it on brand

Do
  1. Start every layout from near-black and add only what earns its place
  2. Let one blue element carry the meaning of the screen
  3. Set headlines light, wide and large; set data in mono
  4. Show real screens, real drawings, real project numbers
  5. Give the hero lines and the $5B+ figure their own space
  6. Use hairlines and surface steps for depth
  7. Name the pathway the visitor is on — persona or asset class
  8. End on the URL
Don't
  1. Add a second accent colour or any purple-to-cyan “AI” gradient
  2. Round corners past 10px or drop shadows on cards
  3. Set a headline in bold, condensed or italic type
  4. Illustrate AI as a face, brain, orb or robot
  5. Fake a product capability to fill a layout
  6. Stack three CTAs where one belongs
  7. Animate the logo or the whole page at once
  8. Write “seamless”, “revolutionary” or “AI-powered”
