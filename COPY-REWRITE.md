# Biltflow homepage — copy rewrite

Rewrite of the live copy in `Design 1/extracted/v3.dc.html`.
Structure, section order and the visual system are unchanged — this is copy only.

**Reader assumption driving every call below:** Australian builder-developer, 45–60,
non-technical, on site, arriving from a LinkedIn message or a referral. They do not read.
They look. Three seconds to understand the page.

**Three constraints held throughout:** no invented customers, no invented metrics, no
invented product capability. Nothing below adds a claim that was not already on the page
or already in the locked script.

## 1 — Hero

**Eyebrow** `HIGH-DENSITY RESIDENTIAL` — unchanged.

**H1** (unchanged, approved)
> ## The tools changed.
> ## The way construction operates didn't.

**Subheadline — rewritten.**

Current: *The AI-native operating system for high-density residential development and construction.*

That is a category definition, not a benefit. It tells the reader what Biltflow **is** and
nothing about what they can now do. It also fails the "Now you can" test — you cannot complete
the sentence "Now you can…" from it. It repeats the eyebrow, and it leads with the one word the
brief says to quarantine.

- **Option A (recommended)** — *One record for the whole build — site sourcing to defects — so the variation, the RFI and the programme finally agree.*
  Lifts "none of them agree with each other" straight from the practitioner quote already on the
  page. Names the loop's two ends so the ring beside it is immediately legible. No "AI".
- **Option B** — *Thirteen stages of a build, held in one record that gets sharper with every project.*
  Shorter, sets up both the ring and the compounding section. Slightly more abstract.
- **Option C** — *The AI-native operating system for high-density residential development and construction.*
  Unchanged. Use if the locked script's wording must survive verbatim pending Raj's sign-off.

> **Flag:** annotation 2 in the brief records that Raj argued against both "AI-native" and
> "for construction" on the 26 Aug call. A and B resolve that objection; C preserves the script.
> This is a client decision, not a copy decision.

**CTA** — *Book a demo* (unchanged as primary; see §CTA note at the end).

## 2 — The noise

**Heading** (unchanged) — *More software. More data. More complexity.*

**Stat line — rewritten.**

Current: `12 SYSTEMS, 0 RECORDS OF TRUTH`

"Records of truth" is vendor language. A site manager does not use it. "Single source of truth"
is also the most worn phrase in enterprise software, and the half-version reads as a typo.

> **Rewrite:** `TWELVE SYSTEMS. NOT ONE OF THEM AGREES.`

Concrete, and it is the reader's actual daily experience rather than a data-architecture concept.

## CTA note

Every CTA on the page is *Book a demo*, and the brief fixes it at one CTA, sales-gated. Keep it.

But there is a mismatch worth flagging to Raj rather than fixing silently in the copy: RFI-04
states plainly that Biltflow has not yet run a full lifecycle on a live job, and there are no
product screenshots anywhere on the page. A reader who has just absorbed that is being asked to
book a demo of something the page has carefully avoided showing.

RFI-04 already describes what actually happens on that call — *"scope it against your project:
which stage you would start with, what condition your data is actually in, who owns it
internally"*. That is a stronger and more honest offer than a demo.

- **Option A** — *Book a demo* — unchanged. Familiar, and the brief's stated choice.
- **Option B** — *Scope it against your project* — matches what the call actually delivers, and
  reads as lower-commitment to a sceptical buyer.
- **Option C** — *Walk us through your job* — lowest friction, flatters the reader's expertise,
  and puts them rather than the product at the centre.

Recommend testing B against A once there is traffic. This is a positioning decision, so it is
Raj's call, not a copy fix.
